\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We built an open, locally runnable criterion-level eligibility screener around a LoRA-adapted Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, evaluated it against its unadapted base on identical held-out patients, and implemented our own KV-cache decoder, tiled attention and speculative decoder with measurements on a CPU. Fine-tuning made a 1.5B open model a usable criterion-level screener, with 0.73 test accuracy, always-valid structured output and sentence-level evidence. Its main weakness is clinically important: it treats missing evidence on exclusion criteria as proof of eligibility, a bias inherited from the label distribution, which future work should counter with rebalancing or an explicit evidence guard. On the systems side, KV caching is the one optimisation that pays off on a laptop CPU. Tiled attention buys memory rather than speed, and speculative decoding is exact but only helps when outputs are long and verification is cheap. Future work: independent multi-seed training, larger external evaluation, and GPU benchmarks of the same code.
